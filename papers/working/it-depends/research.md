## Load-Bearing Claims to Pressure-Test

### Claim 1: Experience provides hypotheses, not prescriptions

**Support needed:**
- Naturalistic decision making literature (Klein) — what does it actually say about transferability?
- Expertise research — when does pattern recognition fail?
- Consulting-specific research on expert failure modes

**Potential counterevidence:**
- Klein's Recognition-Primed Decision model shows experts make correct decisions rapidly in many domains
- Some research may show that expertise transfers better than this claim suggests

**Falsifier:** If research shows expert pattern recognition is reliable across significantly different organizational contexts, this claim needs revision.

---

### Claim 2: Similarity is not equivalence

**Support needed:**
- Case studies where similar situations differed in critical ways
- Research on contextual factors in organizational change
- Examples from other domains (medicine, aviation, etc.)

**Potential counterevidence:**
- Best practice transfer success stories
- Meta-analyses showing certain practices work across contexts

**Falsifier:** If meta-analyses show that specific practices succeed at high rates across diverse contexts, this claim needs qualification.

---

### Claim 3: Act proportionally to reversibility and consequence

**Support needed:**
- Decision theory literature on evidence thresholds
- Real options reasoning
- Safety science on risk assessment

**Potential counterevidence:**
- Research showing that fast action often outperforms deliberation
- Lean startup / agile research on bias toward action

**Falsifier:** If research consistently shows that deliberation based on reversibility/consequence performs worse than heuristics like "bias toward action," this claim needs revision.

---

### Claim 4: Allow reality to update the model

**Support needed:**
- Double-loop learning (Argyris & Schön)
- Bayesian updating
- Research on defensive reasoning and its effects

**Potential counterevidence:**
- Research on self-fulfilling prophecies
- Studies where persistence despite negative feedback led to success

**Falsifier:** If research shows that updating models based on outcomes performs worse than committed persistence, this claim needs revision.

---

### Claim 5: The consultant changes the system by arriving

**Support needed:**
- Observer effect in social systems
- Research on intervention effects in organizational studies
- Hawthorne effect literature

**Potential counterevidence:**
- Cases where consultant presence had minimal effect
- Research on objective assessment methods

**Falsifier:** If research shows that skilled consultants can observe without affecting the system, this claim needs qualification.

---

### Claim 6: Some relational state is unknowable

**Support needed:**
- Research on tacit knowledge
- Social network analysis limitations
- Psychology of hidden emotions/attitudes

**Potential counterevidence:**
- Research showing that skilled interviewers can uncover relational dynamics
- Network analysis methods that reveal hidden structures

**Falsifier:** If research demonstrates reliable methods for uncovering all materially relevant relational state, this claim needs revision.

---

### Claim 7: A useful model implies predictions

**Support needed:**
- Philosophy of science (Popper on falsifiability)
- Research on predictive vs. explanatory models
- Decision theory on actionable beliefs

**Potential counterevidence:**
- Research on retrospective sensemaking (Weick)
- Cases where non-predictive models were still useful

**Falsifier:** If research shows that non-predictive models are equally or more useful in organizational contexts, this claim needs revision.
