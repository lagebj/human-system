# Corpus-Backed Voice Workflow

## Principle

Do not invent the author's voice from a style guide.

Retrieve evidence of it.

The corpus does not replace Human System conceptual context, evidence discipline, public-language principles, or surface-specific judgment.

## Drafting

Before important reader-facing Human System prose:

1. Determine the cognitive surface, not just the artifact type.
2. Retrieve 4–8 relevant primary corpus samples from the closest surfaces.
3. Read complete passages, not isolated "signature" lines.
4. Retrieve relevant rejected-AI evidence.
5. Prefer a current author-authored seed: note, message, question, rough paragraph, or phrase.
6. Recover the actual thought before creating structure.
7. Draft by extending the author's seed where possible.
8. Let structure emerge after the thought has started moving.

Do not begin with a generic article skeleton and attempt to add voice later.

## Comparison pass

Before polishing, compare against the retrieved samples:

- Did ordinary connectors become formal transitions?
- Did repetition become synonym variation?
- Did accumulation become taxonomy?
- Did uncertainty become generic hedge language?
- Did directness become diplomatic padding?
- Did shorthand become inflated abstraction?
- Did questions become rhetorical marketing hooks?
- Did emotion become neutral?
- Did an unresolved thought gain an automatic conclusion?
- Did a concrete case become a general principle too quickly?
- Did the draft become balanced in ways the source language is not?
- Did the linguistic skeleton come from the author seed or from a generic LLM essay?

If the skeleton is generic, re-author from the seed. Do not "humanise" the existing AI skeleton.

## Editing

Editing should improve legibility where necessary without normalising the idiolect.

Do not assume:
- repeated word = defect;
- uneven sentence = defect;
- long sentence = defect;
- short compression = defect;
- unfinished intellectual tension = defect.

Do assume:
- reader confusion about the actual thought may require revision;
- factual claims still require evidence;
- public context may require expansion that chat did not.

## Feedback loop

When the author says:
- "this sounds like me" — consider adding the surrounding passage with provenance;
- "this is too AI/processed/polished" — preserve representative negative evidence;
- "this is too clean" — compare the draft against raw samples before inventing new style rules.

Do not silently harvest every interaction.
