# Thought Trail Policy

## Purpose

Raw samples help answer: **How does the author naturally express a thought?**

Thought trails help answer: **What happens when the author stays with a thought?**

A thought trail is evidence of intellectual movement across multiple direct-author fragments.

## Primary evidence

Only direct author language belongs in fragment sections of a `direct-user-message sequence`. Do not paraphrase missing fragments, reconstruct wording from summaries, insert assistant prose between fragments, or clean grammar/spelling/punctuation/repetition.

Derived metadata is allowed outside fragment bodies when clearly marked.

## Retrieval

For substantial Human System writing retrieve: (1) the current author-authored seed, (2) relevant raw idiolect samples, (3) 2–4 thought trails selected by topic and/or cognitive movement, and (4) a long-form depth reference when appropriate.

Cross-domain trails are allowed when cognitive movement is relevant. They are evidence of thinking patterns, not evidence for domain claims.

## Privacy

Before committing a new trail, exclude or redact confidential client data, private third-party information, secrets, and internal identifiers. Preserve syntax where practical. Do not commit reconstructed “quotes” from memory summaries.

## Growth

Prefer 5–15 connected fragments over large collections of isolated one-liners. A trail is valuable when the sequence teaches something the raw corpus cannot.
