# Contextual Intervention — Current Conceptual Context

**Status:** Working context. Internal. Not a paper, not a manifesto, not a framework.

This document holds the current direction of the wider body of thought the
repository exists to develop. It is written for agents and collaborators who
need to understand what the project is currently thinking about, at what level
of confidence, before they help with concept work, research, or paper drafting.

It is not operationally authoritative. Nothing here governs how work is done in
the repository. It informs reasoning; it does not decide it. The operational
rules live in `docs/decisions/002-repository-operating-model-and-source-authority.md`
and `AGENTS.md`.

Read this as observations, hypotheses, and propositions held at different
confidence levels, not as settled results. Where the thinking is unsettled, the
unsettledness is part of the content. Do not manufacture coherence that has not
been earned.

---

## Subject

How to understand and intervene responsibly in human systems when the right
action depends on context.

The recurring entrypoint is the consultant, and more broadly the person doing
technical and organizational work who repeatedly enters systems that existed
before them and will continue after them, while being expected to provide
answers because of prior experience. The subject is not consulting itself.
Consulting is the vantage point, not the topic.

---

## Working propositions

Held with varying confidence. Each is open to established theory,
counterevidence, and revision.

### On "it depends"

- "It depends" is not the end of an answer.
- Stronger form: it depends, therefore identify what it depends upon.
- Context comes before prescription.
- Experience should improve the quality of questions and hypotheses. It does not
  remove the need to understand the current context.
- Prior resemblance provides hypotheses, not automatic prescriptions.

### On entering a system

- A consultant becomes part of the system immediately.
- Observation itself can alter the system.
- Discovery is therefore also intervention.
- Assume in proportion to reversibility and consequence.
- Intervene in proportion to understanding, reversibility, and consequence.

### On the limits of knowing

- Context can never be completely known.
- Material variables may be hidden or inherently inaccessible.
- The system always contains more reality than our model of it.
- Abstractions are useful. They become dangerous when treated as authoritative
  over the reality they represent.

### On relationships

- Relationships are infrastructure. More precisely, living infrastructure.
- They carry information and trust, and also history, attraction, love, affairs,
  dislike, resentment, jealousy, admiration, fear, embarrassment, obligation,
  loyalty, status, and private interpretation.
- Some relational state may materially affect the work while being completely
  invisible to an observer.
- Even a relationship graph is an abstraction. Moving from formal structure to
  relational structure gets closer to reality without capturing it completely.

### On agency and conditions

- People exercise agency inside conditions they did not wholly create, while
  their actions continuously reproduce and modify those conditions.
- Management has responsibility for conditions within its meaningful sphere of
  influence.
- Practitioners retain responsibility for their own conduct, communication,
  relationships, judgment, curiosity, and participation.
- "Management owns the ecology. Practitioners own their conduct within it"
  remains useful shorthand. It must not imply that management literally controls
  every contextual condition.
- "Specify the edges. Leave the middle adaptive" remains a useful design
  principle where uncertainty warrants adaptive execution.

### On control and information

- Controls are not inherently bad.
- Control becomes problematic when it substitutes for judgment or corrupts
  information.
- Fear and perceived consequence can create an organizational observability
  problem by changing what people are willing to report.

### On friction as method

- Deliberate short-term suboptimization can sometimes expose hidden workflows,
  dependencies, competence gaps, ownership gaps, workarounds, assumptions, and
  organizational debt.
- Friction must not automatically be rationalized after the fact as learning.

### On feedback and revision

- Interventions should contain predictions or expectations that reality can
  challenge.
- When outcomes contradict expectations, the explanatory model itself must
  remain open to revision.
- Principles often travel more safely than practices. Principles are still
  abstractions and must remain open to contextual challenge.

### On enablement

- Enablement remains relevant. It is now better understood as one possible
  consequence of a good intervention rather than the governing thesis.
- Increasing future capability and reducing inappropriate dependency may be
  desirable outcomes. They are not universal laws detached from context.

### On the Trojan Horse Movement Pattern

- It is one applied pattern arising from particular experience.
- It is not the constitution of the wider body of thought.
- Its hard claims remain open to challenge.
- See `resources/source/field_patterns/trojan_horse_movement_pattern.md` and the
  status note at the top of that file.

### On the book

- The manuscript is one possible future synthesis.
- It is not the container into which all new thinking must be forced.
- Shorter, narrower papers are expected to be the primary near-term way of
  testing ideas.

## Relationship to established traditions

This thinking is not claimed as original where it overlaps existing work. It
plausibly overlaps, at least:

- Deming and the theory of profound knowledge
- systems thinking and system dynamics
- socio-technical systems
- complexity and complex adaptive systems
- cybernetics and second-order cybernetics
- organizational learning
- psychology, including work on safety, defensiveness, and helping relationships

Future research should try to identify:

- where the thinking overlaps established theory
- where the combination or framing is distinctive
- where consultant entry changes the perspective
- where ideas extend or qualify established thinking
- contradictions, both internal and with established work
- boundary conditions
- falsifiable expectations
- evidence that would challenge a specific claim

Until that work is done, treat overlap as likely and originality as unproven.

## What this document must not become

- a numbered method or step model
- a maturity model
- a diagnostic checklist
- a branded or named system
- a rigid epistemic-status database where every sentence carries a label
- a public paper or manifesto

If a future paper draws on this material, the paper may state propositions
directly, explain reasoning, cite literature, discuss counterexamples,
distinguish observation from inference, and expose uncertainty. This document
stays internal and stays provisional.
